# Claim verification on SciFact: can a critic make an LLM judge better?

**Bottom line.** A Gemini judge classifies scientific claims against retrieved abstracts at macro-F1 **0.781**
(κ = 0.668 against the human labels) with no critic at all. The frozen main method, which adds a blind LLM
polarity check and a NOT_ENOUGH_INFO recheck (B1), did **not** improve on that: macro-F1 −0.004 [−0.029, +0.022]
and contradiction recall +0.047 [−0.016, +0.118], neither significant (Holm-corrected p = 1.0). The polarity
checks were slightly *harmful*; the NOT_ENOUGH_INFO recheck alone was the only component with a positive estimate
(contradiction recall +0.062 [+0.014, +0.127]), but it does not survive correction for the 16 declared tests
(Holm p = 0.50). Giving the judge the gold abstracts instead of the retrieved ones changed almost nothing
(+0.010), so retrieval is not the bottleneck: the judge's reluctance to commit is. This is a result of a pipeline
run **once** on the 300 dev claims with a configuration frozen beforehand, reported as it came out.

**How it was done.** The configuration ([results/verify_freeze.json](results/verify_freeze.json), fingerprint
`231a36b8df199e24`) was developed on a **60-claim stratified subset of the train split** (20 SUPPORT, 20
CONTRADICT, 20 NOT_ENOUGH_INFO), committed and pushed, and the analysis plan (primary and secondary comparisons,
Holm correction) was fixed before the dev claims were touched. The dev run, over the 300 BEIR test queries (the
original SciFact dev claims: 124 SUPPORT, 64 CONTRADICT, 112 NOT_ENOUGH_INFO), executed once. Nothing was changed
afterwards, and everything labelled *exploratory* below was computed or written after seeing the results.

## What it does

For each claim: retrieve the top 5 abstracts with the app's default hybrid retriever (BM25 + bge-small, RRF), have
Gemini (`gemini-3.8-flash`, temperature 0, thinking `low`) return a verdict (SUPPORT, CONTRADICT or NOT_ENOUGH_INFO)
with the sentences it cites, then let a **critic** check the answer and, on failure, retry once with specific
feedback (LangGraph: judge → critic → accept | retry, at most two attempts). Labels and gold rationale sentences
come from the original SciFact release; the 300 BEIR test queries are exactly its 300 dev claims (checked on every
run: 300 of 300 ids and texts identical, same 5,183 documents).

The critic has three independent parts, which are run as separate **arms** so each one's effect can be isolated:

| Arm | Judge + … |
| --- | --- |
| B0 | nothing (no critic) |
| B3 | citation rules: every cited sentence must exist, and a decisive verdict must cite something |
| N | rules + **NEI recheck** (below) |
| B1p | rules + a *blind* LLM polarity check |
| B2p | rules + a blind NLI polarity check (DeBERTa-v3, no LLM) |
| B1 | rules + LLM polarity check + NEI recheck |
| B2 | rules + NLI polarity check + NEI recheck |

The polarity check is *blind*: it sees only the claim and the sentences the judge cited, never the verdict or
rationale, so it cannot simply agree with the judge. It reports whether those sentences entail, contradict or are
neutral to the claim, and the critic compares that with what the verdict asserts. **Faithfulness** is the share of
SUPPORT / CONTRADICT verdicts whose cited sentences entail the verdict according to this check. A separate arm A
(NLI alone, no LLM) and an oracle arm (the judge given the gold abstracts) complete the comparison.

### Faithfulness and cited evidence

Faithfulness is the share of SUPPORT / CONTRADICT verdicts whose cited sentences entail the verdict, according to the
blind polarity check. It needs a checker, so B0 and the oracle have none.

| Arm | Faithfulness, first answer | Faithfulness, final answer | Cited-sentence P / R / F1 vs human rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B0 judge only | n/a | n/a | 0.64 / 0.54 / 0.59 |
| B1p + LLM polarity | 0.954 (146/153) | 0.993 (147/148) | 0.65 / 0.52 / 0.58 |

The faithfulness number is only as good as its checker, which was validated against the human-marked evidence

| Checker | Items | Contradictions recovered | Entailments recovered | Mismatched controls called neutral |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LLM | 188 gold + 60 controls | 0.750 (n=64) | 0.637 (n=124) | 1.000 (n=60) |
| NLI | 188 gold + 188 controls | 0.641 (n=64) | 0.540 (n=124) | 0.904 (n=188) |

The LLM checker never calls a mismatched pair "entailing" but misses a third of the human-marked entailments, so it
is conservative: it can wrongly *flag* a correct answer (which is what happened in the 6 harmed cases), and its
faithfulness figures are likely conservative too. That the cited sentences overlap the human rationale only
moderately (precision about 0.64, recall about 0.54) is partly because the judge's false NOT_ENOUGH_INFO answers
cite nothing.

### Retrieval ceiling versus the oracle

On the 188 claims that have evidence, the retriever puts a gold evidence document in the top 1 for 77.1% and in the top
5 for 93.1%. If retrieval were the bottleneck, handing the judge the gold abstracts would help a lot. It does not:

| | Macro-F1 | κ | SUPPORT recall | CONTRADICT recall | NEI recall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| B0 (retrieved top 5) | 0.781 | 0.668 | 0.74 | 0.69 | 0.89 |
| Oracle (gold abstracts) | 0.791 | 0.683 | 0.71 | 0.69 | 0.96 |

The oracle gain is +0.010 [−0.026, +0.047] (exploratory), with no change in CONTRADICT recall and a *drop* in
SUPPORT recall. The judge is the limit, not retrieval.

### Exploratory: anatomy of the baseline's errors

*Computed after seeing the results; not declared in advance.*

| Gold \ predicted | SUPPORT | CONTRADICT | NOT_ENOUGH_INFO |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SUPPORT | 92 | 4 | 28 |
| CONTRADICT | 1 | 44 | 19 |
| NOT_ENOUGH_INFO | 6 | 6 | 100 |

47 of the 188 claims that have evidence were answered NOT_ENOUGH_INFO, and for 37 of those a gold evidence document
was in the retrieved top 5. Only **5** claims had SUPPORT and CONTRADICT swapped, the error a polarity check targets,
while 12 NOT_ENOUGH_INFO claims got a decisive verdict. So the dominant error (a missed verdict, 47 claims) is one
the polarity checks cannot see, and the one they can see (5 swaps) is too rare to repay their cost. The NEI recheck
is aimed at the right error, but its NLI signal is too noisy to separate the 47 wrong NOT_ENOUGH_INFO answers from
the 100 right ones.

Other exploratory comparisons (uncorrected, in the full summary): the abstain policy hurts the NLI-critic arms
badly (B2p − B0 macro-F1 −0.075 [−0.113, −0.040]); the LLM critic beats the NLI critic under that policy
(B1 − B2 macro-F1 +0.055 [+0.017, +0.094]); B3 equals B0 exactly.

What this does and does not show:

- **The judge's decisive verdicts were all correct on this subset** (precision 1.00 for SUPPORT and for

  judge makes no polarity errors, and both polarity-only arms scored *below* the judge alone (−0.017, one harmed

- **The only improvement comes from the NEI recheck:** +0.032 macro-F1 over rules alone (95% CI [0.000, 0.082]),

- **The LLM checker is a far better polarity judge than the NLI model**, measured against human-marked evidence

  entailments (n = 40, accuracy 0.85) and calls 100% of shuffled-claim negative controls neutral (n = 20). The NLI

  Faithfulness of the judge's decisive verdicts: 0.968 (30 of 31) by the LLM checker, 0.839 (26 of 31) by NLI,

- Cited sentences versus the human rationale (40 evidence claims): precision 0.67, recall 0.51 (recall is
  depressed by the false NOT_ENOUGH_INFO verdicts, which cite nothing).

## The NOT_ENOUGH_INFO recheck rule

Every judge error on the train subset was a false NOT_ENOUGH_INFO that cites nothing, which neither the citation
rules nor the polarity check can see. The rule works from the other side: when the verdict is NOT_ENOUGH_INFO, an NLI
model scores every retrieved sentence against the claim; if one entails or contradicts it with probability above τ,
the verdict is flagged and the judge retries with feedback naming that sentence. The threshold was chosen on the
train subset by the pre-stated rule *maximise fixes − harms subject to fixes > harms; drop the rule if none
qualifies*, evaluated offline from the stored retry outcomes at the most permissive threshold:

| τ | Flagged | Fixed | Harmed | Net |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0.5** | 23 | 2 | 0 | **+2** |
| 0.7 | 20 | 1 | 0 | +1 |
| 0.9 | 14 | 1 | 0 | +1 |
| 0.95 | 11 | 1 | 0 | +1 |
| 0.98 | 9 | 1 | 0 | +1 |

Of the judge's 29 NOT_ENOUGH_INFO verdicts, 9 were wrong and 20 right. The rule qualified at every threshold and
τ = 0.5 won, so it stays in the frozen pipeline. Read it carefully: it is a **weak discriminator**. At τ = 0.5 it
flagged 23 verdicts of which only 8 were wrong (precision 0.35), because topically close sentences score high even
for correctly unanswerable claims. It did no harm here only because the judge, asked to reconsider, kept every one of
the 15 correct NOT_ENOUGH_INFO answers that were flagged. The benefit is 2 claims of 60.

A single prompt revision, judge-v2, was written after judge-v1's errors were seen, aiming at the false-NEI bias, and

**A prompt revision did not fix the NEI bias; in 6 of the 8 remaining errors the gold evidence was retrieved at

## Budget and operational notes

Spending limits are enforced in code (`agentrag/verify/budget.py`), not by intention: all API calls stop at Rs 600
of tracked spend or 2,000 requests, spend is persisted per API key, every in-flight request reserves its worst-case
cost before it is sent, a single call costing more than $0.02 stops everything, and each phase of the study has its
own rupee cap. Every response is cached, and a cached prompt is never sent again. Incidents worth knowing about: a
free-tier project allowed only 20 requests per day and stopped the first attempt (the client recognised the quota and
stopped cleanly); a Windows file-lock error while saving the spend ledger once discarded one paid response before it
was cached (one prompt was paid for twice, fixed with a regression test); and a too-tight cost tripwire halted the
train run when retries began thinking (tripwire since set to $0.02). The frozen dev run then completed in one pass
with no stop, no tripwire and no resume.

## Limitations

- **Gemini may have seen SciFact.** The dataset has been public since 2020, and nothing here can rule out that
  `gemini-3.8-flash` saw its claims, abstracts or labels in pretraining. That could inflate the judge's absolute
  numbers (macro-F1 0.781) in a way no experiment on this data can measure. It is less likely to distort the
  *differences* between arms, which all share the same judge, but that is an argument, not a test.
- **The contradiction count limits power.** With 64 CONTRADICT claims, a 95% interval on contradiction recall is about
  ±0.07, and macro-F1 intervals are about ±0.02–0.04. Effects smaller than roughly 0.05–0.06 cannot be resolved, so
  "no significant improvement" does not mean "no improvement"; it means none large enough to see. The N − B0 effect
  (+0.062 contradiction recall, raw p = 0.034) sits right at that edge and did not survive correction for 16 tests.
- **One run, one model, one configuration.** Temperature 0 does not guarantee bit-identical API output, one prompt
  and one retrieval setting (top 5, hybrid) were tried on dev, and only a single judge model was evaluated. The
  response cache is committed, so every number here can be re-derived offline (`--offline`) without any API call.
- **Tuning on 60 claims.** The NLI thresholds were tuned on the full train split, but the NEI-recheck threshold
  (0.5) and the choice of judge prompt rest on a 60-claim subset, where one claim moves macro-F1 by about 0.015.
- **SciFact's NOT_ENOUGH_INFO is not "nothing exists".** It means the abstract a claim was written about had no
  evidence. In an open corpus, some claims labelled NOT_ENOUGH_INFO may be supportable from other abstracts, so some
  decisive verdicts on them (12 here) may be defensible, which would understate every arm slightly.
- **The faithfulness metric is only as good as its checker.** The LLM checker recovers 64% of human-marked
  entailments and the NLI checker 54%, so faithfulness is measured with an imperfect instrument. The 0.954 and 0.719
  figures should be read as relative, not absolute.
- **The critic's reach.** It can only challenge a verdict that cites a sentence (the polarity check) or a
  NOT_ENOUGH_INFO that an NLI score can contradict (the recheck). It cannot notice evidence the judge neither cited
  nor surfaced, and that is where the baseline's main error (47 missed verdicts) lives.
- **Costs are list-price estimates** from token counts, not billed amounts.

## What this does and does not show

It shows that, with a judge this strong, adding a blind polarity check does not help (the judge rarely gets the
direction wrong: 5 swaps in 300 claims) and can hurt, and that a cheap NLI-based recheck aimed at the real error (an
unwarranted NOT_ENOUGH_INFO) moves in the right direction but is too noisy to be established at this sample size. It
does not show that critics cannot help: a weaker judge, a better recheck signal, or a larger evaluation could change
the picture. The pipeline, cache and pre-registered analysis make that a cheap thing to test.
